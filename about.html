About Me

Curious by nature. Always figuring something out.

Hi, I'm Hibah! I'm an Information Technology student at George Mason University with a minor in Psychology. I've always been a curious person and I like figuring out how things work, whether that's something technical or even just understanding why people think and act the way they do.

IT and psychology are pretty different subjects, but that's actually part of why I enjoy studying both. I like having the technical side where I can solve problems and work with different systems, while psychology lets me explore people, behavior, and different ways of thinking.

My Story

How I Got Into IT

My interest in technology started way before I actually thought about it as a career. I grew up playing games and spending a lot of time on computers, which naturally made me curious about the technology behind them. I was also the kind of kid who would take apart a broken fan or device because I wanted to see what was inside and try to fix it. Sometimes I successfully put it back together. Sometimes... not so much.

For a while, I actually thought I would go into psychology. I still really enjoyed it, but as I started exploring different subjects, technology just clicked with me more.

My first real experience working in IT was at Fairfax County Public Schools. I got to work with actual devices and troubleshoot problems instead of only learning about technology in a classroom. I helped with things like laptop imaging, inventory, hardware and software issues, and preparing devices for students.

I also noticed that whenever we had to escalate an issue, I was curious about what happened after it left our hands and how someone at a higher support level would solve it. That's when I started realizing I wanted to keep going further into IT.

Since then, I've realized that troubleshooting is probably one of my favorite parts of the field. I like getting a problem where I don't immediately know the answer and having to work through it until something finally clicks.

Psychology + Technology

Different subjects, but more connected than I expected.

Even after deciding to focus on IT, I didn't want to completely give up psychology, so I kept it as my minor. I genuinely enjoy learning about how people think, communicate, and approach situations differently.

The more experience I've gotten in IT, the more I've noticed that the two can overlap too. Working in support means you're not only dealing with a computer or a piece of software. You're also working with a person who might not understand what's happening or might explain a problem completely differently than another user would.

That's made communication a really important part of IT for me. I like figuring out the technical side of a problem, but I also like finding a simple way to explain what happened and what we're doing to fix it.

What I Enjoy

Troubleshooting

I like problems that make me stop and think for a little bit. Troubleshooting gives me a chance to test different ideas, rule things out, and eventually figure out what was actually causing the issue. Getting something working after being stuck on it is probably one of the most satisfying parts for me.

02

Learning as I Go

I definitely don't expect myself to already know everything. I've gotten pretty comfortable being given something I'm unfamiliar with and learning it as I go. I'll research it, ask questions when I need to, try things out, and eventually it becomes something I'm comfortable working with.

03

Hands On Work

I learn a lot better when I can actually work with something. Reading about a tool or concept is useful, but actually using it and running into problems along the way is usually when I understand it best.

04

Different Perspectives

One thing I like about psychology is seeing how differently people can look at the exact same situation. I think that's also made me more open to hearing how other people approach problems, especially when their solution is completely different from the way I would have done it.
